## Design Context

### Users
Young urban professionals (25-40) who are tech-savvy, design-conscious, and treat their pets like family. They use the app at home on their phones and sometimes on desktop at work. They expect polished, modern UX that feels as premium as the pet care products they buy (Wild One, Jinx, The Farmer's Dog).

### Brand Personality
Warm + Trustworthy + Premium. Not cold luxury — warm premium. Like a high-end veterinary practice that's still approachable and caring. The interface should feel like a carefully designed physical object: intentional materials, thoughtful details, nothing wasted.

### Aesthetic Direction
Inspired by premium pet brands (Wild One, Jinx, The Farmer's Dog) rather than generic SaaS pricing pages. Dark theme with warm amber/gold accents. Clean typographic hierarchy. Solid surfaces over excessive glassmorphism. Restrained use of color — let the gold accents breathe. Editorial approach to layout: asymmetric where it makes sense, plenty of breathing room, intentional spacing rhythms.

### Design Principles
1. **Subtract before adding** — Every element must earn its place. Remove anything decorative that doesn't serve clarity or emotion.
2. **Warmth through material** — Use solid backgrounds with warm undertones instead of glass/frosted effects. Premium feels solid, not ephemeral.
3. **Typography as architecture** — Let font weight, size, and spacing do the work. Fewer decorative elements, more typographic contrast.
4. **Intentional asymmetry** — Break the grid deliberately to create visual interest, not randomly. The hero card should feel like a hero.
5. **Motion with purpose** — One well-orchestrated entrance is better than scattered micro-interactions. Use motion to guide attention, not distract.

### Interaction and Copy Standards
- **Motion brief:** Before adding an animation, state the user benefit, the start and end states, what properties move, and the timing/easing. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`: remove decorative or ongoing movement and use a fade or color shift when motion communicates a useful state change. “No animation” is a valid decision.
- **Form errors:** Name the field, explain what went wrong, and give the correction when known. Match the wording to the field label and keep entered answers after validation fails. Separate user-input errors from service or appointment-availability failures, and give service failures a clear next step. For several validation errors, pair inline messages with a summary that links to them.
